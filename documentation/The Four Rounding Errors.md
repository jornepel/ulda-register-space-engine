The Four Rounding Errors
Operations Beyond the Compass

Document identifier: ULDA-READER-2026-X1
Parent document: ULDA-MATHEMATICS-2026-X7 (We Do Not Compute With Zeros)
Status: Companion reader. Identifies four operations the compass cannot represent.
Author: Jorne Pel
1. The scope boundary

The compass is a specific system. It represents:

    Directions (vectors in ℤ[φ]³).

    Rotations (the icosahedral group I, order 60).

    Loops (the pentagon, order 5).

    Axes (irrep 3, three directions).

It does not represent:

    Coordinates (exact rationals like 1/2).

    Cardinalities (variable counts).

    Primitives (rank 0, the triangle).

    Ratios (comparisons of forces).

When the compass encounters one of these, it rounds. The rounding is not a failure — it is a boundary marker. The rounding tells you that the puzzle is outside the compass's domain.

The four unassignable puzzles are four rounding errors. Each one is the compass rounding a general operation to a specific one. The rounding is the artifact. The artifact is the boundary.
2. Riemann — locating

The artifact: 1/2.

The operation: finding the coordinate of the mirror axis. The critical line Re(s) = 1/2 is the exact midpoint of the critical strip (0 to 1). Not almost half — half.

What the compass has: the mirror axis (rank 2 → rank 1). The compass can represent that there is an axis.

What it can't represent: the coordinate 1/2. The compass works in ℤ[φ] and integers. 1/2 is a rational. The compass rounds 1/2 to the nearest ℤ[φ] value.

The rounding: the coordinate 1/2 → rounded to ℤ[φ].

The verb: locating. Riemann finds the coordinate of the mirror axis. The compass has the axis; Riemann has the coordinate. The rounding is the coordinate.
3. BSD — counting

The artifact: variable rank.

The operation: counting the independent generators of the elliptic curve. The rank is the number of independent points. It can be 0, 1, 2, 3, … — any non-negative integer.

What the compass has: the axes (irrep 3) — a fixed set of three directions.

What it can't represent: the cardinality. The compass's irrep 3 is exactly 3, not "any number." The compass rounds the variable rank to 3.

The rounding: the variable rank → rounded to 3 (the fixed axes).

The verb: counting. BSD counts the independent generators. The compass has three axes; BSD counts any number. The rounding is the count.
4. Hodge — looping

The artifact: the primitive (rank 0).

The operation: closing loops. The Hodge decomposition (data science) splits an edge flow into gradient, curl, and harmonic. The curl is local circulation around triangles — the primitive, rank 0. The triangle is part 1 of the dodecahedron: 6 triangles per pentagon.

What the compass has: the pentagon (rank 1) — the specific loop, order 5, φ-saturated.

What it can't represent: the primitive. The compass's loop is rank 1 (the pentagon); the Hodge decomposition stays at rank 0 (the triangle). The compass rounds rank 0 up to rank 1.

The rounding: the primitive (rank 0) → rounded to rank 1 (the pentagon).

The verb: looping. Hodge loops at the primitive. The compass loops at the specific. The rounding is the rank.
5. Hierarchy — comparing

The artifact: 10⁻³².

The operation: comparing two forces. Gravity (central, irrep 3) and the weak force (chiral, irrep 3′). The hierarchy is their ratio.

What the compass has: the forces (irrep 3 and irrep 3′). The compass can represent the forces.

What it can't represent: the ratio. The compass is a rotational system; the ratio is a comparison, not a rotation. The compass rounds the ratio to a force (irrep 3 or 3′).

The rounding: the ratio 10⁻³² → rounded to a force (irrep 3 or 3′).

The verb: comparing. Hierarchy compares two forces. The compass has the forces; Hierarchy has the ratio. The rounding is the ratio.
6. The pattern

Each artifact is the general version of a specific compass operation. The compass has the specific; the artifact is the general. The compass rounds the general to the specific.
Puzzle	Verb	The compass has	The artifact is	The rounding
Riemann	Locating	The mirror axis	The coordinate (1/2)	1/2 → ℤ[φ]
BSD	Counting	The axes (irrep 3)	The cardinality (variable rank)	rank → 3

Four verbs. Riemann locates. BSD counts. Hodge loops (at the primitive). Hierarchy compares.

Four roundings. Each is the compass rounding a general operation to a specific one. The rounding is the artifact. The artifact is the boundary.
7. The finding

The compass is exact in its own domain. It does not round directions, rotations, loops, or axes. Those are its specific objects.

But when it encounters a general operation — a coordinate, a cardinality, a primitive, a ratio — it rounds. The rounding is not a failure. It is a boundary marker. The rounding tells you that the puzzle is outside the compass's domain.

The four unassignable puzzles are four rounding errors. Each one is the compass rounding a general operation to a specific one. The rounding is the artifact. The artifact is the boundary.

That is the result.
